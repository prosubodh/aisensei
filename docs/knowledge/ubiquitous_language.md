## 1. Pedagogical & Cognitive Vocabulary

### Active Recall
The cognitive practice of retrieving information from memory through effortful testing rather than passive review. Significantly enhances neural path stabilization and long-term consolidation.

### Bloom's Taxonomy
A hierarchical ordering of cognitive skills: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create. Mastery requires graduating beyond passive comprehension into analysis and creation.

### Chunking
The mental process of grouping individual pieces of information into larger meaningful units, reducing cognitive load on limited working memory.

### Cognitive Dissonance
The psychological discomfort experienced when encountering evidence that contradicts an existing mental model. The essential catalyst for conceptual growth.

### Desirable Difficulty
Learning conditions that require focused effort and slow down initial apparent progress, but produce significantly higher long-term retention and flexible transfer.

### Elenchus (Socratic Refutation)
The method of cross-examining an interlocutor's claims to reveal internal contradictions, thereby dismantling false confidence and establishing first principles.

### Feynman Technique
The method of testing mastery by teaching a concept in elementary language without reliance on technical jargon, exposing hidden knowledge gaps.

### Learning Mastery Record (LMR)
An immutable, machine-verifiable ledger entry recording an assessed concept, score, evidence, and retention decay schedule.

### Spaced Repetition
A scheduling algorithm (based on Ebbinghaus forgetting curves) that spaces out retrieval checks at exponentially expanding intervals (1, 3, 7, 14, 30 days).

### Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD)
The optimal learning zone between what a learner can accomplish unaided and what they cannot accomplish even with guidance.
